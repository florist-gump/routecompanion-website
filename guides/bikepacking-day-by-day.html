Bikepacking day by day

Split the trip, find a bed, sort the food

On this page

A multi-day trip is planned one day at a time: how far you ride, where you sleep, and where you get food in the evening and the next morning. This guide goes through each of those, and how to set them up in Route Companion.

Pick a day you can repeat

Plan for a day you could ride again tomorrow, and the day after, on a loaded bike. That's usually well short of your longest day out. Bags slow you down, gravel slows you more, and stops get longer once you're living out of the bike.

If you're not sure, plan shorter than feels right. Finishing at four leaves time to find the campsite, shower, eat and look around. Finishing in the dark leaves none of that.

In the app, open the route and tap Split into days. You can split three ways:

  • Distance, for a fixed number of kilometres a day (80 km to start with).
  • Riding time, for a fixed number of hours on the bike. This one is kinder on a hilly route, where 80 km can mean a short afternoon or a very long day.
  • Days, when the dates are already fixed and the distance has to fit them.

Each day shows its distance, climbing and riding time. The times come from your own usual speed and stops, so set those first (the gear next to Split into days takes you there).

Let the bed decide where the day ends

The split ends each day wherever the kilometres run out, and that's rarely next to a bed. Take it as a first guess, then pick the nights.

Tap Choose nights, or the moon marker at the end of a day. The map jumps to that spot and lists hotels, guest houses, hostels and campsites within 2 km, the closest first, with how far each one is before or past the day's end. Nothing there? Tap Widen to 5 km, or open the search chip and go further. If you're carrying a bivvy, you can add shelters to the search too.

Pick one, tap Sleep here, and move on to the next night.

When the bed is off the route

The nice campsite is often down by the lake, 3 km off your line, and those 3 km are usually downhill on the way there and uphill the next morning. They belong in your plan.

When the place is more than 50 m from the route, the app asks how the day ends. Ride to the door adds the way there to that day, and the next day starts at the door and rides back to the route. End on the route keeps the day on the line and leaves the detour out of the numbers.

Dates, bookings and the closed campsite

Turn on Start time and each day gets a date and an arrival time at your night's stop. That's the time to check against: campsite receptions often close in the evening, and a lot of campsites close for the whole winter.

On busy summer weekends, book the first night and keep the rest loose. Note a second option for each night from the list, so you can change your mind at lunch.

Food and water around the night

You roll into the village at seven and the one shop shut at half six. Or it's Sunday, and nothing opens until you're 40 km down the road. Evenings and mornings are where food runs short on a trip.

So when you save the trip, each day becomes its own route. Open a day and search along it for supermarkets, bakeries, cafés or water, and the app shows whether each one will still be open when you get there. For a campsite night, buy dinner and breakfast at the last shop before it, and fill your bottles there too.

Save it, then change it

Tap Save trip. You get one route per day, all together in one collection under History, so you can ride each day on its own. Plans change on the road, so Edit trip brings the whole split back: move a night, ride a longer day, and save again.
